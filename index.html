Color palette — five

Night and stone carry every role: they swap base and text between modes, both fully legible either way. Charcoal lifts cards off the night base. Teal and sand are fills only, never text, since a midtone can't hold contrast against both a dark and a light background: put night text on accent blocks. Nothing outside these five is used. Muted text, hairlines, and light-mode card surfaces are the dark or stone color at reduced opacity, not new tints.

Tracked uppercase labels

Hierarchy comes from size, weight, and case, not color. Dark and charcoal are intentionally close, so text never relies on the color gap to separate: body is Regular 400, headings step up to Medium 500, captions step down in size, and labels are Medium 500 caps with wide tracking.

Do

  • Pair night and stone as base and text; flip them between modes.
  • Use teal and sand as fills: blocks, badges, graphic shapes.
  • Always put night text on a teal or sand fill.
  • Let teal lead as the primary accent, sand support.

Don't

  • Set teal or sand as text: a midtone fails on one background.
  • Use teal and sand at equal weight; they compete.
  • Drop to pure white or pure black; keep the warm neutrals.
  • Add color beyond the five. The set is deliberately tight.
